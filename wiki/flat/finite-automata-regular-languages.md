# Finite Automata and Regular Languages

**Subject:** FLAT
**Prerequisites:** None
**Exam Importance:** High
**Last updated:** 2026-05-12
**Sources:** [FLAT Comprehensive Study Materials - Kerala Notes](../sources/source-flat-comprehensive-study-materials-kerala-notes.md)

## Definition

A regular language is a language accepted by a finite automaton, described by a regular expression, or generated by a regular grammar.

## Core Facts

- Every finite language is regular.
- DFA and NFA have the same expressive power.
- Converting an `n`-state NFA to a DFA can require up to `2^n` states.
- Minimal DFAs are unique up to renaming of states.
- Mealy and Moore machines are equivalent in expressive power.
- Two-way finite automata still accept exactly the regular languages.

> **[EXAM_TIP]** Fixed finite-position constraints, such as "the 10th symbol from the right is 1", are regular because a DFA only needs finite memory.

## Closure

Regular languages are closed under union, intersection, complement, concatenation, Kleene star, reversal, homomorphism, and inverse homomorphism.

> **[CONNECTION]** Regular-expression syntax and SQL pattern matching connect loosely to DBMS [SQL and Query Languages](../dbms/sql-and-query-languages.md), but FLAT regular expressions are formal language objects with exact automata semantics.

## Common State Counts

- Last `k` symbols constraint: often `2^k` states.
- Divisibility by `m`: usually `m` remainder states, with possible minimization.
- Simultaneous modular constraints: product construction, e.g. `300 x 200 = 60000`.
