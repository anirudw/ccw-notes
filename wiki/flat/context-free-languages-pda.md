## Definition

A context-free language is generated by a context-free grammar and accepted by a pushdown automaton. A PDA adds a stack to finite control, which lets it count and match nested structure.

## PDA Facts

- NPDA and DPDA are not equivalent in power.
- Every DCFL is a CFL, but not every CFL is deterministic.
- PDA acceptance by final state and by empty store are equivalent for CFLs.
- A marked palindrome language such as `{wcw^R}` is deterministic context-free.
- Unmarked palindromes `{ww^R}` are CFL but not DCFL.

> **[EXAM_TIP]** A middle marker is often the difference between a DPDA solution and a nondeterministic PDA solution.

## CFL Closure

CFLs are closed under:

- union
- concatenation
- Kleene star
- homomorphism
- inverse homomorphism
- intersection with regular languages

CFLs are not closed under:

- intersection
- complement
- difference

## Grammar Notes

- CNF rules are of the form `A -> BC` or `A -> a`, with standard start-symbol exceptions.
- GNF rules begin with a terminal.
- Linear grammars generate a proper subset of CFLs, but all regular languages have linear grammars.
